# Adjust to the scope

Use these distinctions to select follow-up questions. Do not read every question aloud or assume a job title grants authority.

**One person runs the skill at every level.** Scope describes whose work is being explored. A team representative can discuss department-wide opportunities; one leader or staff member can explore organization-wide work. Neither needs to assemble a group to begin. Their account remains one perspective.

Each level supports a wide scan ending in a shortlist or focused work ending in a small next step. Do not force a pilot when the participant only wants ideas.

## Individual

Focus on work the participant actually does and the outcome they care about. A staff member or volunteer can explore ideas without speaking for their organization.

Understand their current experience with AI, access to permitted tools, available time, and practical concerns. They may want help understanding material, preparing a draft, practicing, or checking quality rather than automating anything.

Useful follow-up: “Which part of that could you try differently without changing anyone else's work?”

Output: a shortlist across their work, a personal practice exercise, an approved task trial, or one specific permission to seek. An individual trial still needs appropriate data use and a way to check the result.

## Department or team

In a wide scan, explore different departmental activities, handoffs, learning needs and desired improvements through the one participant's account. In focused work, follow one shared piece of work: who initiates it, who does it, who checks it, and who uses the result. Learn where a change would move work onto someone else.

Useful follow-up: “Who would need to help us check whether this actually makes the task easier?”

One person's account is a starting point. Identify whose perspective is missing; do not write “the team agrees” without evidence. Preserve disagreements as different accounts. Do not share private individual concerns with a manager without that person's agreement.

Do not assign an absent or dissenting colleague to an AI exercise without their willingness being established. Mark possible participation as unresolved, or identify a role to consult. A reported reservation is not evidence of either agreement or a permanent opt-out.

Output: a varied shortlist with relevant perspectives still to consult, or a proposed trial with the people doing and reviewing the work, the relevant decision-maker, and capacity identified. Unknowns can remain alongside speculative ideas; resolve the necessary ones before a live trial.

## Organization

In a wide scan, explore several mission and operating activities through the participant's account, including work they want the organization to become able to do. Keep the scan about the organization's work rather than a list of products. For a chosen idea, establish which functions and people are affected and whose knowledge is missing. Staff, volunteers, leaders, partners, and the people served may have different interests; identify relevant perspectives without requiring a committee simply to explore ideas.

Useful follow-up: “Whose work would change first if we tried this?”

Locate actual ownership of decisions about tools, information, funding, and service quality. Do not assume an executive can override board, funder, contractual, data, or professional requirements. Ask only about requirements that are relevant to the proposed task; do not invent a compliance checklist.

Output: a shortlist of different opportunities or one pilot proposal and the next decision needed, with missing perspectives and assumptions visible. It is not a completed organizational AI strategy or approval to adopt.

## If a helper or another person contributes

Keep one designated participant for the interview. If a helper types for them, distinguish the participant's answer from the helper's interpretation when it matters. Team and organizational modes are not facilitated group workshops.

If someone else briefly contributes, clarify whose account it is and who will see any shared take-away before eliciting personal concerns. If the audience is unknown, keep to non-sensitive task descriptions. Honor exclusions and make no anonymity promise. Do not treat their presence, manager's approval or contribution as agreement to an AI exercise. Return to the designated participant's exploration without combining private accounts automatically.
