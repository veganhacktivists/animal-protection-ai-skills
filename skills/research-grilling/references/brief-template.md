# Research brief template

Use these headings in this order. Fill each with what was decided and, for judgement calls, why. If a section has nothing in it, write "Nothing decided yet" so a reader can tell it wasn't forgotten.

```markdown
# <Project name>: research brief

<Client> · Version of <date> · Status: <"All decisions settled" or "Waiting on the client for N questions">

## What the research is for
The decision the client will make with it, who will read it, and how they will read it.

## Scope
Countries, time period, species, products or sectors, and what is deliberately left out.

## Prior work we are building on
What already exists (the client's, ours, other people's), with links, and what we will reuse instead of redo.

## Foundational concepts to understand first

## Evidence standards

## Data
One entry per dataset: what it is, which objective it answers, who holds it, the licence, how and when we get it, coverage and level of detail, known problems, and the fallback if it falls through. Then any data we collect ourselves (survey, interviews), and how data is stored and handed over at the end.

## Methods
The analysis approach. Then each judgement call as its own line: the choice made, and the reason.

## Constraints
Deadline, budget or days, who does what, and what the client provides.

## The finished product
Format, length, graphs, interim drafts, sign-off.

## Risks
Each risk, with what we will do about it.

## Waiting on the client
Numbered. For each: the question, the working assumption we are using, and what changes if the client says otherwise. Mark answered ones "Confirmed" or "Changed" with the date. Mark ones the client never answered "Not confirmed".

## What to research first
In priority order. Foundational concepts come first, then anything whose answer might need to go back to the client before the interim draft.

## Decision log
Numbered, oldest first. Each line: the decision, who made it (team, client, or assumed), and the date.
```
